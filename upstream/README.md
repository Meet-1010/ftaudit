Each PR's regression test was checked both ways: it fails against the
unpatched code and passes with the fix. Every test waits on an `Event` rather
than a `Barrier` and skips when a runner cannot provide threads, after SymPy's
CI turned up a `RuntimeError: can't start new thread` on a constrained runner.

## Why CPython is a request rather than a patch

The gh-120321 fix series exists on `main` and was never backported. I tested
whether it cherry-picks onto 3.14: **1 of 9 commits applies cleanly**. The
foundational commit is ~1,100 insertions across 16 files including
`Python/bytecodes.c` and the machine-generated bytecode tables, which depend on
post-3.14 changes such as the SEND specialization. That is interpreter-core
work, so the issue presents the evidence and defers the decision to the people
who own the original fix.

## Why the GIL-declaration reports are not filed

"This extension re-enables the GIL" is a fact about a shipped wheel, not a
demonstrated thread-safety bug. The honest ask is "please audit this and then
declare it", which is work for the maintainer rather than a patch they can
merge. Those reports are written up in
[finding 02](../findings/02-gil-reenabled-packages.md) and are better raised
once there is something more than the observation to offer.
